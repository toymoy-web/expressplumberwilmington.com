Water Softener Installation & Repair in Wilmington, NC

Scale buildup on fixtures, a softener that stopped regenerating, or a system you're installing for the first time. We size, install, and repair water softeners for real Wilmington water conditions.

Call (919) 944-7281, 24/7

Water softener installation and repair in Wilmington, NC covers new system sizing and installation, troubleshooting a softener that's stopped regenerating on schedule, and repairing or replacing worn components like the resin tank, control valve, or brine tank.

Whether a softener is genuinely needed, and how it should be sized, depends on the specific water supply for a given address, since well water and municipal CFPUA water can differ meaningfully in mineral content. We test before recommending a system size rather than assume.

Why this happens in Wilmington

Why softener needs vary across New Hanover County

Homes on private wells, more common in parts of unincorporated New Hanover County outside CFPUA's service area, often deal with harder water and sometimes iron content that municipal customers don't see. That changes both whether a softener is worth installing and how it should be configured.

For homes on municipal CFPUA water within Wilmington itself, softening needs are usually about scale prevention and fixture longevity rather than a dramatic water-quality problem, a different conversation than the one we have with well-water customers.

Signs to watch for

Signs a softener needs service or installation

  • Visible white or chalky scale buildup on faucets, showerheads, or glassware.
  • Soap that doesn't lather well or leaves a filmy residue.
  • A softener that's stopped regenerating on its normal schedule.
  • Unusually high salt usage or a brine tank that isn't drawing down.
  • Considering a softener for the first time due to fixture wear or appliance scale.
How we find and fix it

How we approach a softener job

For a new installation, we test the actual water supply for hardness and, on well systems, other minerals like iron, rather than size a system off a generic assumption. That test result determines the softener's capacity and regeneration settings.

For a system that's stopped regenerating, we check the control valve's timer or metered settings first, since a misconfigured schedule is a common and inexpensive fix compared to a resin bed problem.

If the resin bed itself is fouled or exhausted, which shows up as consistently hard water even right after a regeneration cycle, that's a resin replacement rather than a settings fix, and we'll say so plainly.
