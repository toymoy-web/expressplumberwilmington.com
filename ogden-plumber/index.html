Ogden, NC 28411 is a census-designated place in New Hanover County with about 8,200 residents at the 2020 census, up from 6,766 in 2010. It sits between Porters Neck to the north, the Middle Sound Loop area in the middle, and the gated community of Landfall to the south. Downtown Wilmington is roughly a 15 minute drive away.

Most of Ogden has been built out since the late 1990s, so the plumbing here is newer than what we see in the historic downtown blocks. That changes the usual calls. Instead of corroded galvanized lines, we more often find failed fittings, water heaters that have already been replaced once, and drains stressed by landscaping.

We dispatch to Ogden from our Wilmington base, and non-emergency estimates are free. Call (919) 944-7281 to schedule, or use the emergency line at any hour.

A newer suburb on low coastal ground

Ogden sits close to the Intracoastal Waterway on low, flat coastal ground. After a stalled tropical storm or a week of heavy rain, the soil stays saturated and water looks for the lowest point. Crawl spaces and low yards feel that first.

That is why drainage plumbing matters more here than the age of the homes suggests. A clogged yard drain or a failed sump pump can put water under a house within hours. Our Sump Pump Repair page explains what we check and how fast we can respond.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for Ogden and the neighboring 28405, 28403, and 28480 ZIP codes, from the Market Street corridor south to Wrightsville Beach. Leak detection and drain cleaning are the two services we are called for most often across that stretch.

Market Street, Ogden Park, and the places our trucks pass

Market Street, also signed as Business 17, is Ogden's commercial spine, lined with grocery stores, pharmacies, and restaurants. Kitchens along that strip put steady grease loads into their drain lines, which is a different problem from a home bathroom drain. Our Hydro Jetting service is the usual answer for a line that keeps backing up.

Ogden Park draws families with its dog park, tennis courts, picnic areas, and walking trails. The residential streets around it are mostly single-family homes on established lots. Ogden Elementary on Middle Sound Road sits in the same neighborhood fabric.

The Lewis Strawberry Farm and a handful of other small businesses sit farther out along the road network. Properties like these often run long outdoor water lines and hose bibs, which are the first places a freeze or a ground shift shows up.

Middle Sound Loop and the waterfront edge

Toward Middle Sound Loop and the Intracoastal, lots get larger and homes get more varied. Some properties on the edge of the area may use septic systems instead of public sewer. We ask early which system a home is on, because a slow drain means very different things on each.

Landfall, the gated community to the south, is part of the same market. Gate access and community rules are routine for us, and we confirm both before we head out so the visit starts on time.

What fast growth means for older and newer homes alike

Ogden grew about 21 percent between 2010 and 2020, and new phases keep going up. Builder-grade fixtures installed during a rush sometimes fail early, so we see more warranty-period surprises here than in an older neighborhood. Mentioning that your home is a recent build when you call helps us bring the right parts.

Homes from the first wave of building in the late 1990s are now at the age where their first water heater is long gone and supply fittings are due for a look. We handle both kinds of calls from the same truck.
