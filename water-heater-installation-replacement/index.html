Emergency Water Heater Replacement in Wilmington, NC

Replacing a water heater is a decision you make once every eight to twelve years. We help you get the size, type, and placement right the first time.

Call (919) 944-7281, 24/7

Water heater installation and replacement in Wilmington, NC is a different job from a water heater repair. A repair fixes one failed part on a tank that's otherwise sound. A replacement means the old unit is at or past the end of its useful life, and the real decision is what to put in its place, not just how to fix what's there.

Most Wilmington homes replace a tank water heater with another tank system. A growing number ask about switching to tankless at replacement time instead, since that's the natural moment to consider a different setup rather than adding cost to a system you'll replace again in a few years anyway.

Emergency response

Emergency water heater replacement when the tank has failed

When a tank splits open or a heater is too corroded to repair, waiting is not an option. A flooded utility closet, a garage floor covered in water, or a family without hot water calls for a fast replacement, not a long quote cycle.

Call the emergency line and tell us the size of your current unit and its fuel type. We quote the replacement before work starts, keep the job to a single visit when the right unit is on the truck or available locally, and haul away the old tank.

Before we arrive: what to do about a failed water heater

  • Shut off the cold water supply to the heater to stop more water from entering the tank.
  • Turn off the electric breaker or gas control so the unit does not run without water.
  • Clear the area around the tank so the plumber has room to work and remove the old unit.

Why this happens in Wilmington

Why replacement timing matters here

Wilmington's humid subtropical climate shortens the practical life of a steel tank compared to a drier inland market, since constant ambient moisture accelerates corrosion on external fittings and the tank's lower seams. A unit rated for 10 to 12 years elsewhere may be closer to 8 to 10 here.

For homes with a water heater in a crawl space, especially in the newer slab-foundation neighborhoods further from downtown, replacement is also the right time to talk about elevation. Coastal New Hanover County sees real crawl-space flooding risk during heavy rain and hurricane season. Setting a new unit higher on a stand, or moving it to a garage or conditioned space where that's practical, is a one-time decision that a repair never gives you the chance to make.

Signs to watch for

Signs it's time to replace, not repair

  • The tank is actively leaking from the shell, not a fitting.
  • The unit is 8 or more years old and needing repairs more than once a year.
  • Rust-colored water from hot taps that doesn't clear after running for a minute.
  • The household has consistently outgrown the tank's hot water capacity.
  • You're renovating and want to reconsider tank versus tankless.

Replacing a water heater that has flooded or failed

Many replacement calls begin with water on the floor. If the old tank has leaked, we shut off the supply, drain it, and look at the pan, drain line, and surrounding area before installing the new unit.

Tell us whether you have an electric, gas, or hybrid heat pump unit and how large it is. That lets us bring the right replacement and finish the job in one visit whenever stock allows.

Neighborhoods and landmarks

Replacement jobs from Landfall to Brunswick Forest

Large homes in Landfall and Porters Neck Plantation often need a bigger tank or two heaters to cover several bathrooms. In Leland communities such as Brunswick Forest and Compass Pointe, newer slab homes use heaters installed in garages or utility closets.

Downtown, a replacement may need a pan, a drain line, and updated venting in a house that predates modern code. We plan the install around the building.

How we find and fix it

How we help you choose

We size a replacement tank to the household's actual usage, not a generic same-size swap, since a family's hot water needs often change over the years a water heater is in service.

For anyone considering tankless, we talk through the real trade-off honestly: a tankless unit costs more upfront and may need a gas line or electrical upgrade, but delivers continuous hot water and typically lasts longer. Once one's installed, see our tankless water heater service page for the flushing and maintenance schedule that keeps it performing.

Installation includes properly connecting to existing gas, water, and venting lines, or updating them where code requires it, and testing the full system before we consider the job done.
