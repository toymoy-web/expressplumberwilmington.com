Plumber in Carolina Place, Wilmington, NC

Carolina Place is a compact streetcar-era neighborhood east of downtown Wilmington, known for Craftsman bungalows, mature hardwoods, and Wallace Park.

Call (919) 944-7281

If you own a home in Carolina Place, you already know the neighborhood is a mix of well-kept bungalows and houses that have been updated one room at a time. That mix is where most plumbing calls come from. A kitchen redone twenty years ago sits next to a bathroom that has never been touched.

Wilmington Express Plumbing takes calls from Carolina Place for leaks, slow drains, sewer line trouble, and water heater problems. When we arrive, we start by finding out what is actually in your walls and under your floor, not by assuming what a house of this age must contain.

What we can verify about Carolina Place

Carolina Place covers about 46 acres east of downtown. Market Street forms the northern edge, Wallace Park the eastern edge, Gibson Avenue the southern edge, and the diagonal Wrightsville Avenue the southwest edge.

It was Wilmington's first streetcar suburb, started by the American Suburban Corporation in 1906, and it was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1992. Most homes are Craftsman bungalows built between 1906 and the Second World War, with some Late Victorian cottages. Four larger Classical Revival and Queen Anne houses stand along Market Street between 17th and 18th streets.

We do not have records on the pipe materials or sewer lines under any individual street, so we do not guess at them. What we can say is that the age range of the housing means repairs and remodels have likely happened at different times in different homes.

Trees, a creek, and drain lines

Carolina Place has mature hardwood trees, and Burnt Mill Creek runs through Wallace Park on the eastern edge. Large trees near a home are a common cause of slow main drains, because roots seek out moisture at pipe joints. This is general plumbing knowledge, not a finding about any specific street.

If a main drain backs up more than once, a camera inspection of the sewer line shows whether roots, a belly in the pipe, or a collapsed section is the cause. That answer decides whether cleaning is enough or a repair is needed. Our hydro jetting and sewer line pages explain both options.

Practical advice for older bungalow plumbing

Know where your main water shutoff is before you need it, and test that it turns. A valve that has not moved in years can seize, and that is the worst moment to learn it.

If you are remodeling a bathroom or kitchen, ask for a look at the existing supply and drain lines first. Replacing a fixture onto old piping can work well, but it is better to decide that on purpose than to find out after the wall is closed.

Helpful details to have ready when you call

Tell us which fixtures are affected, when it started, and whether the main shutoff is closed. A photo of the leak or the water heater label helps. If you live in one of the larger houses along Market Street, mention how many bathrooms and floors are involved, because that changes how we plan the visit.
