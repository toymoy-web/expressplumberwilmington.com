Plumber in Sunset Park, Wilmington, NC

Sunset Park is a historic neighborhood about three miles south of downtown Wilmington along Carolina Beach Road, built out from the 1910s to the 1960s.

Call (919) 944-7281

Sunset Park grew in stages. The earliest houses date to the 1910s, and the cross streets filled in with Cape Cod and ranch houses in the 1940s and 1950s. A single street can hold homes built decades apart, each with its own plumbing history.

Wilmington Express Plumbing serves Sunset Park for water heaters, drains, leaks, and fixture repairs. We look at what each house actually has before suggesting a fix.

What we can verify about Sunset Park

The historic district is roughly bounded by Carolina Beach Road, Southern Boulevard, Burnett Boulevard, and Sunset Avenue. Lots went on sale in 1912, and the district covers about 227 acres of a much larger original tract.

Listed styles include Queen Anne, Bungalow or Craftsman, Tudor Revival, Colonial Revival, Cape Cod, and ranch. The earliest houses sit along Northern and Central boulevards, and the 1940s and 1950s Cape Cods and ranches line the cross streets. The district was added to the National Register on December 10, 2003.

We do not have information on foundation types or pipe materials for individual streets, so we check on site.

Mid-century houses and everyday wear

Many Sunset Park homes are mid-century Cape Cods and ranches. Houses of that size usually have one main water heater, a few bathrooms, and shared drain lines, so a single failing part can affect the whole house. Tank water heaters have a limited service life, and a tank that is rusting at the seams or leaking at the base needs replacement rather than repair.

If your water heater is more than ten years old and you have noticed rust-colored hot water or pooling near the base, it is worth having it inspected before it fails. That is general guidance and not a finding about any one home.

Simple checks you can do

Look at your water bill. A sudden rise with no change in how you live is often the first sign of a hidden leak. Turn off all fixtures and see whether the meter still moves.

Check under sinks and around toilets for dampness a few times a year. Small seepage at a supply line is cheap to fix and expensive to ignore.

Helpful details to have ready when you call

Note the approximate age of the water heater, which fixtures are affected, and whether you have seen rust or moisture near the base. If you know whether your house sits on a slab or over a crawl space, say so. If you do not, that is fine, because we check it when we arrive.
