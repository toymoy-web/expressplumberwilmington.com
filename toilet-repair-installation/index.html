Toilet Repair & Installation in Wilmington, NC

A running toilet, a rocking bowl, or a flush that just doesn't clear anymore. We fix the actual cause, not just the symptom, and install a new toilet correctly the first time.

Call (919) 944-7281, 24/7

Toilet repair and installation in Wilmington, NC covers the full range. That includes a running toilet wasting water, a wobbling bowl that's actually a failed wax ring, a fill valve that won't shut off, or a full replacement when a toilet is simply worn out or you're renovating a bathroom. If the water is showing up somewhere other than right at the toilet's base, that may point to a separate leak detection issue in the bathroom's supply lines instead.

Most repair calls are one of a handful of common failures, a flapper that no longer seals, a fill valve stuck open, or a wax ring that's broken down and letting water seep at the base. Each has a specific fix, and most don't require replacing the whole toilet.

Why this happens in Wilmington

Why toilets fail differently in older vs. newer Wilmington homes

In older homes near downtown Wilmington, a rocking or leaking toilet is often actually a floor or subfloor issue. New Hanover County's sandy soil and the settling common in decades-old foundations can shift a toilet's flange slightly out of level, which breaks the wax ring seal even though the toilet itself is fine.

In newer slab-foundation homes further from downtown, toilet problems are more often a straightforward component failure, a fill valve or flapper reaching the end of its service life, rather than a foundation-related issue. Knowing which pattern applies to a specific home changes whether the fix is a simple part swap or a proper reset of the toilet on the flange.

Signs to watch for

Signs of a toilet problem worth calling about

  • The toilet runs intermittently or continuously after flushing.
  • The bowl rocks slightly when you sit down or lean on it.
  • Water seeping out at the base of the toilet onto the floor.
  • A weak flush that doesn't fully clear the bowl.
  • The tank refills slowly or the fill valve makes a whining noise.
How we find and fix it

How we diagnose and fix it

A running toilet is almost always the flapper or the fill valve. We check both, since replacing the wrong one is a common reason a "fixed" running toilet starts again within weeks.

A rocking toilet gets pulled and inspected at the flange, not just re-caulked around the base. If the wax ring has failed, we reset the toilet on a fresh seal. If the flange itself is cracked or sitting below the finished floor, that gets addressed too, since a new wax ring on a bad flange will fail again.

For a new installation, we confirm the flange height and rough-in measurement match the new toilet before it ever goes in, which avoids the common mismatch problem that leaves a toilet sitting slightly wrong from day one.
