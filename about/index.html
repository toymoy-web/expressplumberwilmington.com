About us

Plumbing built around how Wilmington homes actually work

We're a licensed North Carolina plumbing contractor based in Wilmington, and we built this business around the coastal soil, older pipe materials, and hurricane-season realities that a generic plumbing checklist skips over.

Why we focus on the local specifics

Wilmington's sandy coastal soil, humid subtropical climate, and mix of decades-old downtown housing with newer slab-foundation subdivisions each create different plumbing failure patterns. A slab leak in a 2005-built home near the airport has different likely causes than a sewer backup in a pre-1960 home near downtown. We diagnose accordingly instead of running the same checklist on every call.

Plumbing contractors in North Carolina are licensed through the State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors, the state agency created in 1931 to license the trade. We operate under that same licensing framework, and our work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code and International Plumbing Code standards the trade is built on nationally.

How we diagnose before we quote

We don't guess and cut. For anything that isn't a visible, obvious fix, we use the right diagnostic step first. That might be a water meter test for a suspected leak, a camera inspection for a recurring drain clog, or simply asking enough questions on the phone to know what to bring in the truck.

That diagnostic step is what lets us give you a real, specific price instead of a wide range, and it's also what keeps us from recommending a bigger repair than the problem actually needs.

Coverage area

We serve Wilmington and the surrounding New Hanover County area. That includes every ZIP code that makes up the city itself: the historic downtown core near the Cape Fear River, the UNCW area, the neighborhoods near the airport, and the Porters Neck area to the north. See our full service area page for details on each part of the county.

Getting ready for hurricane season

Coastal Carolina plumbing means planning around storm season, not just reacting to it. The City of Wilmington's Building Department specifically recommends checking backflow prevention and drainage before hurricane season each year, and we build that advice into how we talk with customers every spring.

  • Backflow preventer testing before the season's first named storm.
  • A clear main water shutoff location every homeowner should know.
  • Crawl-space and sump pump checks before heavy rain season.

What we don't do

We don't quote a flat repair price before seeing or diagnosing the problem, and we don't leave a job unexplained. If a fix can wait, we'll tell you that. We won't upsell emergency service you don't need.

  • No fabricated "years in business" claim: this is a newly established local plumbing service.
  • No invented reviews, star ratings, or customer counts.
  • Free estimates for non-emergency work; upfront pricing before any emergency work begins.

Industry standards we build to

Plumbing work in North Carolina follows the Uniform Plumbing Code and International Plumbing Code, the two model codes local jurisdictions across the country adopt and enforce. We don't claim a certification we don't hold. We do build every repair and installation to the code standard that actually applies, and we'll tell you plainly if a job needs a permit pulled with the City of Wilmington or New Hanover County before work starts.
