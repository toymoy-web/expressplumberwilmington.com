Whole-Home Repiping in Wilmington, NC

When the same house keeps producing new leaks in different spots, the pipe material itself is usually the real problem. Repiping replaces it before the next leak finds you first.

Call (919) 944-7281, 24/7

Repiping means replacing a home's supply piping, the lines carrying water to fixtures, rather than patching one failed section. It's a bigger job than a single leak detection and spot repair, and it's the right call once a home's piping is failing in more than one place.

This is different from repairing the buried water service line running from the street or meter to the house, and it's also different from a sewer or drain line problem covered on our drain cleaning and sewer line repair pages. Repiping is about the plumbing inside the walls and under the floors, not the lines carrying water or waste to and from the property.

Why this happens in Wilmington

Why older Wilmington homes are candidates for repiping

Homes built before the 1990s in the downtown Wilmington area often still run on galvanized steel or early copper supply lines. The area's humid, salt-laden coastal air accelerates corrosion on both, from the inside out. A galvanized line corrodes internally over decades until its interior diameter narrows, which shows up as gradually falling water pressure long before an actual leak appears.

PEX has become the more common replacement material here specifically because it resists the corrosion pattern that eventually pinholes aging copper and galvanized lines in this climate. It's also more forgiving of the soil movement described on our leak detection page, since it flexes slightly rather than cracking at a rigid joint.

Signs to watch for

Signs a home needs repiping, not another spot repair

  • More than one pipe leak in the past year or two, in different parts of the house.
  • Water pressure that has gradually dropped over several years.
  • Discolored water, especially a rust tint, from multiple fixtures.
  • Visible green or white corrosion buildup at exposed pipe joints.
  • A home built before the 1990s that has never had its supply lines replaced.

Why homeowners call about repiping

Callers asking about repiping usually describe repeated leaks, discolored water, low pressure, or an inspection that flagged old galvanized or poly pipe. Some also ask about CPVC that has turned brittle.

We inspect the visible pipe, test pressure, and recommend the smallest repair that will hold. If the system is failing in several places, a whole house repipe may cost less over time than another round of patches.

Neighborhoods and landmarks

Which Wilmington homes most often need repiping

Houses in the Carolina Heights, Brookwood, and Westbrook-Ardmore historic districts were built in an era when galvanized steel supply lines were standard. Decades of mineral buildup narrow those pipes and lower pressure long before they leak.

On Kure Beach and Carolina Beach, salt air adds to the wear on older lines. A full repipe often costs less over time than repeated patch repairs.

If you're asking about "why do I keep getting leaks"

Most homeowners don't come to us asking for repiping by name. They call because they've had two or three unrelated-seeming leaks in the past year and are starting to wonder if it's really unrelated. It usually isn't.

When the underlying pipe material itself is failing, spot repairs buy time between leaks, not an end to them. Repiping is the fix that actually stops the pattern rather than treating each leak as its own isolated event.

How we find and fix it

How a repipe actually happens

We map the full existing supply system first, noting which sections are galvanized, copper, or already PEX from a prior partial repair. That way the plan accounts for the home's real plumbing history, not a clean-slate assumption.

Work typically happens in sections to keep water available to at least part of the house during the project, with access points opened in a small number of targeted spots rather than removing every wall in the home.

Once new lines are run, every fixture is pressure-tested before we close up any access point, so a hidden connection issue gets caught before drywall goes back up, not after.

When we won't recommend a full repipe

If a home has had exactly one leak and the rest of the system tests sound, we won't push a whole-home repipe you don't need yet. A single isolated failure is usually a spot repair, not a sign the whole system is going.
