Garbage disposal repair and installation in Wilmington, NC covers jammed units, leaks at the sink flange or discharge pipe, units that hum without spinning, and full replacement when a disposal has reached the end of its service life.

A disposal that hums but doesn't spin is almost always jammed, not electrically dead, and often fixable in minutes with the right approach. A disposal that's leaking is a different problem entirely and usually means a worn seal rather than anything jammed inside.

Why this happens in Wilmington

What actually causes most Wilmington disposal calls

Grease is the single most common cause of both disposal jams and the downstream kitchen-line clogs described on our drain cleaning page. Grease that goes down warm exits liquid, then cools and hardens further down the line, at the disposal's discharge pipe or in the kitchen drain itself, where it catches other debris.

Fibrous foods, celery, corn husks, onion skins, are the other frequent cause of a jammed disposal, since their fibers wrap around the spinning components rather than getting cut cleanly. Neither cause is specific to Wilmington's climate or soil, but they're worth naming since they're genuinely preventable with different kitchen habits.

Signs to watch for

Signs your disposal needs attention

  • The disposal hums when switched on but doesn't spin.
  • Water pools under the sink near the disposal unit.
  • A burning smell from the disposal after running it.
  • Unusual grinding or rattling noise during operation.
  • The disposal trips the circuit breaker repeatedly.

How we find and fix it

How we handle a disposal call

For a humming disposal, we check for a physical jam first, often a utensil or fibrous food caught in the grinding chamber, before assuming a motor failure. Most jams clear with the right tool and the reset button, not a new unit.

For a leak, we identify whether it's coming from the sink flange, the discharge pipe connection, or the unit body itself. A flange or connection leak is a straightforward reseal or tighten. A leak from the unit body usually means internal seal failure, which means replacement rather than repair. A dripping supply line to the same sink, separate from the disposal, is covered on our faucet and sink repair page.

If a disposal trips the breaker repeatedly, that points to a genuine motor issue rather than a simple jam, and we'll say so rather than keep resetting a unit that's actually failing.
