Why Wilmington plumbing is different

Built for coastal Carolina homes, not a national template

Wilmington sits on sandy coastal soil with pockets of Piedmont clay. That soil shifts with seasonal moisture, a real, documented stress on pipes running under crawl-space and slab foundations across New Hanover County. The practical result: cracked joints and misaligned drain lines show up here more often than they would inland. That is exactly why a slab leak or a slow drain in an older Wilmington home deserves a plumber who checks the foundation type before diagnosing the fix.

The area's humid subtropical climate also speeds up pipe corrosion. It also encourages root intrusion from Wilmington's mature hardwood canopy into sewer lines, a pattern independently reported by more than one plumbing company serving this market. If a drain clogs on the same schedule every year, root intrusion, not a one-time blockage, is usually the real cause. That distinction changes whether snaking or hydro jetting is the better call.

North Carolina plumbing contractors are licensed through the State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors, created by the General Assembly in 1931. The Board's very first license, issued June 4, 1931, went to a contractor working right here in Wilmington. It's a detail worth knowing less as trivia and more as a reminder that plumbing licensing in this state has always centered on getting the trade's basics right, the same standard we hold every job to today.

Hurricane season

Getting your plumbing ready before storm season

The City of Wilmington's Building Department advises checking drainage and backflow prevention before hurricane season each year. That advice holds up under real scrutiny. A backflow preventer that hasn't been tested in a while can let storm surge or floodwater push contaminated water back into a home's clean water supply. That's a bigger problem than most homeowners realize until it happens.

  • Have your backflow prevention device tested before the season's first named storm, not after.
  • Know where your main water shutoff is located before you need it in a hurry.
  • Check crawl-space vents and sump pump function ahead of heavy rain, since coastal crawl spaces flood first.
  • Clear gutters and exterior drains so storm runoff doesn't back up against the foundation.
