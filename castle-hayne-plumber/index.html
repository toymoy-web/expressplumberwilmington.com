The aquifer that shares the town's name

The Castle Hayne aquifer, a real, USGS-documented limestone and sandstone formation, is a major freshwater source across much of coastal North Carolina, supplying public water for Wilmington, Jacksonville, and New Bern. It's also the source for many private wells in and around the community it's named for.

Homeowners on a private well drawing from this aquifer deal with water quality considerations municipal CFPUA customers don't, including a real, documented history of saltwater intrusion concern in parts of the aquifer closer to the coast. That's a genuine reason to test well water here rather than assume it matches what a Wilmington municipal customer experiences.

Not every Castle Hayne address is on a well. Some fall within CFPUA's municipal service area. Confirming which applies to a specific property is one of the first things worth knowing before diagnosing a water-quality or pressure issue.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for Castle Hayne and the neighboring 28411 and 28443 ZIP codes, covering north Wilmington and Hampstead along the same US-17 corridor. Water Filtration and Water Softener Installation are the two calls that come up most often for well customers across that stretch.

A mostly newer, affordable community with an older median age

Most Castle Hayne housing was built in the 2000s or later, a newer profile than much of Wilmington proper, closer to what we see in Leland than in the older downtown neighborhoods. Slab foundations are common here as a result.

The area's median age runs a few years above the state average. Home values are notably more affordable than closer to the coast, a combination that draws both retirees and families looking for value within commuting distance of Wilmington.

What a mixed well-and-municipal community means for water quality calls

A water quality or taste complaint gets diagnosed differently depending on which system serves the property. A municipal CFPUA customer with an odd taste is usually dealing with chlorine or chloramine disinfection byproducts. A well customer on the aquifer could be dealing with mineral content, iron, or, in some areas, the documented saltwater intrusion pattern, each requiring a different real fix.

That's why we ask which water source serves your property before recommending anything, rather than apply a one-size answer to what are genuinely two different starting points. Call (919) 944-7281 with questions about your specific property.
