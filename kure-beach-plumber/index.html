Kure Beach, NC 28449 sits at the southern tip of Pleasure Island, just north of the Fort Fisher State Historic Site and the North Carolina Aquarium at Fort Fisher. The town's median age runs well above the state average. Average home values here are also notably higher than in the busier Boardwalk area to the north, both signs of a community built more around long-term ownership than short-term rental turnover.

Established homes mean established plumbing, for better and worse

A community with an older, longer-tenured population tends to have homes where the original plumbing has had decades to age. Sometimes it's been well-maintained and upgraded over the years. Sometimes it's original from a build era when galvanized supply lines and simpler drain configurations were standard.

Higher average home values here also mean homeowners are more likely to invest in a proper fix the first time. A full repipe instead of another patch, a properly sized new water heater instead of a like-for-like swap, rather than deferring maintenance the way an absentee rental owner sometimes does. Near Fort Fisher and the Aquarium, but still a quiet residential town

Despite sitting next to two of the region's biggest tourist draws, the Aquarium and the Fort Fisher State Historic Site, Kure Beach itself stays predominantly residential. Tourist traffic passes through rather than staying. That means our calls here are almost entirely full-time homes and long-term rentals, not the commercial and short-stay mix we see in Carolina Beach.

The Kure Beach Fishing Pier, one of the oldest on the Atlantic Coast, anchors the town's small commercial strip. The handful of businesses nearby face the same real coastal plumbing pressures as the surrounding homes: salt air corrosion and, for older buildings, aging supply lines that have never been fully updated.

What an older, retiree-heavy population means for scheduling

A community with a higher median age often has different scheduling needs than a household full of working adults, more availability for a mid-week daytime appointment, less tolerance for a repair dragging on across multiple visits. We try to accommodate a schedule that actually works for the homeowner rather than default to standard business hours only.

Accessibility matters more here too. If a repair or replacement involves relocating a fixture, we're happy to discuss accessible-height or easier-reach options as part of the same job. A homeowner planning to stay in a coastal home long-term benefits from thinking about that now, rather than during a future renovation.
