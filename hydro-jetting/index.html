Emergency Hydro Jetting in Wilmington, NC

Snaking punches a hole through a clog. Hydro jetting clears the entire inside of the pipe. When a line keeps clogging on a repeating schedule, that difference is what actually stops the pattern.

Call (919) 944-7281, 24/7

Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure water stream to scour the full interior wall of a drain or sewer line, removing grease buildup, scale, and root intrusion rather than just breaking through whatever is currently blocking the flow. It's a more thorough process than a standard drain cleaning, and it's usually recommended once a simpler cleaning hasn't held.

The clearest sign hydro jetting is the right call is a line that clogs again within weeks of being snaked. That pattern almost always means the pipe wall itself is coated with grease or invaded by roots, not that the snake missed something.

Sometimes a cable cannot finish the job. A main line that clogs again within days, a restaurant drain full of grease, or a floor drain that overflows after every clearing needs more than a quick pass. Hydro jetting uses high pressure water to scour the full pipe wall.

Why this happens in Wilmington

Why jetting matters for older Wilmington lines

Older cast iron and clay sewer laterals common in homes near downtown Wilmington have a naturally rougher interior surface than modern PVC, which gives grease and debris more to cling to over time. A snake can punch through the resulting buildup, but it leaves most of the coating in place, ready to catch the next thing that goes down the drain.

Root intrusion from the city's mature hardwood canopy compounds the problem, since roots that have worked into a joint create a rough mass that traps debris even after the immediate clog is cleared. Jetting is the only method that reliably clears both the grease coating and root growth from the full pipe interior in one pass.

Signs to watch for

Signs a line needs jetting, not just snaking

  • A drain clogs again within a few weeks of being snaked.
  • Multiple drains slow down around the same time, suggesting a shared main line issue.
  • A kitchen line with a known history of grease buildup.
  • A sewer camera inspection shows root intrusion or heavy interior buildup.
  • Recurring slow drainage in an older home with cast iron or clay lines.

Why people ask for jetting by name

Callers who ask for hydro jetting usually have a drain that cabling cleared once and then clogged again. Others are restaurants, rental properties, or older homes with heavy buildup that a cable simply pushes through.

We run a camera first when we can. If the line is intact and the buildup is scale, grease, or roots, jetting is a good choice. If the pipe is cracked or collapsed, jetting can make things worse and we will recommend repair first.

If you've never heard the term "hydro jetting"

Most people calling about this don't ask for hydro jetting by name. They describe a drain that got snaked, worked fine for a few weeks, then clogged again in the same spot. That repeating pattern is the real signal, not the terminology.

If that's what's happening, jetting is usually the fix that actually breaks the cycle, since it clears the coating that keeps rebuilding into a new clog rather than just the clog itself.

How we find and fix it

How a jetting service works

We typically run a camera inspection first to confirm jetting is appropriate for the line's condition. That also catches a fully collapsed or severely damaged section, which jetting alone won't fix and which points instead toward sewer line repair.

The jetting equipment then feeds a hose down the line with a nozzle that sprays high-pressure water forward and backward as it's pulled through, scouring the full interior circumference rather than a single path.

A follow-up camera pass after jetting confirms the line is genuinely clear, not just clear enough to flow for the moment.

Local trust

When we won't recommend jetting

If a camera inspection shows a line that's cracked, offset, or collapsed rather than simply coated with buildup, jetting alone won't fix it, and we'll recommend sewer line repair instead of billing for a service that won't hold.

For a genuinely first-time, isolated clog, we won't upsell jetting when a standard snake will do the job for less.
