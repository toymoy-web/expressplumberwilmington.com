Gas Line Installation & Repair in Wilmington, NC

A gas line problem isn't something to guess at. If you smell gas, get out and call your gas utility first. For everything else, from a new line for a range to a suspected leak, we handle it safely and to code.

Gas line installation and repair in Wilmington, NC covers running new gas lines for appliances like ranges, dryers, and outdoor grills. It also covers repairing an existing line that's leaking or damaged, and connecting new gas-fired equipment such as a tankless water heater or a gas furnace.

Any work on a gas line is licensed plumbing work in North Carolina, tested for leaks before it's put into service, and never left as a temporary fix. A gas connection either passes a pressure test or it doesn't, and we don't consider a job done until it does.

Why this happens in Wilmington

Why gas line condition matters in older Wilmington homes

Homes built before the 1980s in the downtown Wilmington area sometimes still have original black iron gas piping, which can develop threaded-joint leaks over many decades even without any external damage. The area's humid coastal air accelerates corrosion at exposed threaded fittings the same way it does on water supply lines. An older gas line is worth a real inspection, not an assumption that it's fine because nothing smells wrong today.

Adding new gas-fired equipment, a generator for hurricane-season backup power, an outdoor kitchen, or a tankless water heater, often means extending an existing line rather than running an entirely new one. That means confirming the existing line and meter can actually support the added load before installation starts.

Signs to watch for

Signs you need a gas line professional, not a DIY fix

  • A rotten-egg smell near an appliance, meter, or gas line, even faint.
  • A hissing sound near a gas line or connection.
  • A gas appliance that won't stay lit or burns with a yellow, flickering flame instead of blue.
  • Dead or discolored vegetation in a patch above a buried gas line.
  • You're adding a new gas appliance and need a line extended.
How we find and fix it

How we handle gas line work

For a suspected leak, if it's safe to do so, shut off the gas supply and get everyone out and away from the area before calling. Do not operate any switches or open flames near a suspected leak. We locate the exact leak point using a combination of pressure testing and gas detection equipment, never guesswork.

For new installations, we size the line correctly for the appliance's BTU demand and the total load already on the existing line, since undersizing a gas line is a real safety issue, not just a performance one.

Every new connection and every repair gets pressure-tested before we consider the job complete, confirming the line holds pressure with no measurable drop over the test period.
