Emergency Burst Pipe Repair in Wilmington, NC

Water spreading across the floor from a burst pipe is one of the few plumbing problems where minutes matter. Shut off the main valve, then call. We respond 24/7.

Call (919) 944-7281, 24/7

A burst pipe in Wilmington, NC is always treated as an emergency call, day or night. Whether it's a supply line that split open, a frozen section that cracked, or an old fitting that finally gave way, the first priority is stopping the water, and the second is fixing the pipe before more damage happens. If the water is coming from a slow, hidden source rather than an obvious break, that's more likely a leak detection call than a burst.

Wilmington's mild coastal climate means most homes aren't built with the same freeze protection you'd see farther inland, which is exactly why the area's occasional hard freezes hit harder than the temperature alone would suggest.

Why this happens in Wilmington

Why burst pipes happen here, even in a mild climate

Winter Storm Gianna brought 5.8 inches of official snowfall to Wilmington International Airport on February 1, 2026. It was the largest snowfall the city had seen in nearly 40 years, and freezing temperatures lingered for days afterward. Events like that are rare here, which is exactly the problem: pipes in crawl spaces, attics, and exterior walls that are never insulated for sustained freezing are the ones most likely to split when a cold snap like that actually arrives.

This isn't a one-time fluke either. Wilmington has seen pipes burst across the city during past cold snaps, including a widely reported cold-weather event that shut down several Monkey Junction businesses and damaged multiple apartment units in one weekend. Outside of freeze events, an aging or corroded supply line, especially the galvanized and early copper lines common in homes built before the 1990s, can also fail suddenly under normal water pressure.

Signs to watch for

What to do the moment a pipe bursts

  • Shut off the main water valve immediately, this stops the flow faster than closing any single fixture.
  • Turn off electricity to any flooded area at the breaker, if you can do so safely without stepping in water.
  • Open a faucet to relieve pressure and let remaining water drain from the line.
  • Move anything valuable away from the water and start soaking up standing water if it's safe to do so.
  • Take photos of the damage before cleanup starts, for insurance purposes.

Burst pipe calls across the coast and the city

Exposed lines in crawl spaces and outdoor hose bibs on Carolina Beach, Kure Beach, and Wrightsville Beach are the usual suspects after a rare hard freeze. A burst pipe in a downtown historic home can be harder to reach behind plaster.

Near Monkey Junction and in Ogden, a burst supply line behind a drywall wall can flood a slab house quickly, so the main shutoff matters.

How we find and fix it

How we handle a burst pipe call

We prioritize burst pipe calls above scheduled work, since active water damage compounds by the minute. Once on site, we confirm the main valve is fully shut and isolate the damaged section so we're not just capping a symptom. A drain or sewer line failure is a different call entirely, covered on our drain cleaning page, so we'll confirm which system is actually involved before quoting anything.

Repair depends on where the break is and what caused it. A single split in an accessible section is often a straightforward cut-and-repair. If the break is one of several recent failures on an aging line, we'll say so honestly rather than treat it as an isolated incident.

If the burst was freeze-related, we'll also point out any other exposed sections in the same crawl space or attic run that are at similar risk before the next hard freeze.

Local trust

What to know about insurance

Homeowner's insurance in North Carolina typically covers sudden, accidental damage from a burst pipe, which is different from a slow leak or gradual wear. Document the damage with photos before cleanup and check your specific policy for the details.

We'll give you a written description of the repair and cause, which most insurers ask for as part of a claim.
